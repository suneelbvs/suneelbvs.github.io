1 · A buried cavity changed the druggability verdict

The HIF-2α PAS-B domain contains an unusually large internal cavity (~290 Å³) in a hydrophobic core. In the apo state, structured waters occupy the volume; ligand binding displaces them with only modest local rearrangement. That matters because a static crystal structure can make the pocket look inaccessible even while protein breathing makes it kinetically accessible.

2 · The molecule does not need to touch DNA to inhibit a transcription factor

What the naïve model expects

Block the DNA-binding surface or directly occupy the protein–protein interface. For many transcription factors those surfaces are broad, shallow and difficult for classical small molecules.

What HIF-2α offered instead

Occupy an internal PAS-B cavity. The local perturbation propagates toward the HIF-2α:ARNT interface and destabilizes the heterodimer — an allosteric route to suppress transcription.

Modelling question: once the pocket is known, the important computation is not merely “what docks best?” but “which cavity occupancies and conformations most effectively transmit the allosteric perturbation?”

3 · PT2385: the biology worked, but metabolism set the ceiling

PT2385 carried a benzylic secondary alcohol important to binding. Clinically, that same alcohol became a major phase-2 metabolic liability: efficient glucuronidation, largely associated with UGT2B17, contributed to variable and sometimes insufficient exposure.

Tempting fix

Delete the OH.
This attacks the metabolic liability, but also risks deleting a potency-critical pharmacophore.

Better design hypothesis

Keep the OH, detune its reactivity.
Change the local electronics and conformational environment so the molecule remains potent while becoming a poorer glucuronidation substrate.

4 · Belzutifan: fluorine as a multi-parameter design tool

The redesign moved from the earlier difluoro arrangement to a vicinal 2,3-difluoro, all-cis stereotriad in belzutifan. The point was not “add fluorine because fluorine is good”; it was to place fluorine where it could alter electronics, conformation and lipophilicity while preserving the alcohol.

↓ Glucuronidation

Neighboring fluorination electronically reduced the alcohol’s susceptibility to UGT-mediated conjugation.

↑ Potency

The optimized stereoelectronic arrangement improved binding rather than merely rescuing metabolism.

↓ Lipophilicity / binding

The new property balance reduced excessive plasma protein binding and improved developability.

Multiparameter win

Potency, clearance, CYP profile, cardiac safety and phase-2 metabolism were optimized together.

5 · What happened after approval still teaches computational chemistry

Two later observations sharpen the lesson. First, anemia is expected from HIF-2α biology because HIF-2α regulates erythropoietin; that is an on-target effect rather than a classic chemical off-target liability. Second, acquired resistance can arise through pocket mutations such as G323E, turning the original druggability story back into a structural and molecular-dynamics problem.

Resistance reframes the pocket: a hydrophobic cavity that enabled the first drug can evolve. The next design objective becomes mutant-pocket compatibility, conformational sampling and retention of allosteric control.

Take-home design principles

Computational chemistry

Look for cryptic pockets, structured water, transient openings and allosteric transmission. Do not let a single static structure define druggability.

Medicinal chemistry

When a liability is attached to a pharmacophore, try to detune the liability rather than delete the feature. Treat stereochemistry and electronics as optimization levers.

DMPK

First-in-class programs can fail for phase-2 metabolism even when potency and target engagement are strong. Glucuronidation deserves explicit screening.

Human judgment

Algorithms iterate inside an objective function. The scarce skill is defining the right objective at each gate: pocket, mechanism, exposure, safety and resistance.
